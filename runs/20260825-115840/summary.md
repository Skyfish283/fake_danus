- **[approach_1]** (approach, by planner) Pseudospectral & Resolvent Norm Analysis for MCMC Classification: Classify MCMC generators L (reversible Langevin, non-reversible Langevin, PDMP) using resolvent operator norm ||(\lambda I - L)^{-1}|| in L^2(pi). Non-normal generators allow fast non-reversible convergence even with small spectral gap, captured by pseudospectra \sigma_\epsilon(L).
- **[approach_2]** (approach, by planner) Implicit/Proximal Resolvent Samplers: Formulate MCMC transition steps as discrete resolvent operators (I - \gamma L)^{-1} or Moreau-Yosida envelopes, enabling stable stepping for unnormalized targets with steep or non-smooth gradients without requiring global posterior normalization.
- **[lemma_1]** (lemma, by planner) Lelièvre-Nier-Pavliotis Spectral Gap Optimization Theorem: For V(x) = (1/2)x^T S x with S > 0, non-reversible linear drift b(x) = -J S x (J^T = -J) preserves the invariant distribution while maximizing min Re(sigma((I+J)S)) = Tr(S)/N. This improves the exponential decay rate in L^2(psi_infty) from lambda_min(S) to Tr(S)/N, governed by resolvent operator norms with a non-normal prefactor bounded by C_N kappa(S)^{7/2}.
- **[approach_4]** (approach, by planner) Local Gram-Schmidt Resolvent Drift Equalization for Unnormalized Targets: Estimate local Hessian S(x) = grad^2 V(x) from target unnormalized gradients, construct state-dependent antisymmetric matrices J(x) via Gram-Schmidt spectrum equalization, and apply proximal resolvent stepping (I - gamma L_{J(x)})^{-1} to optimize local spectral gaps without needing the global normalization constant Z.
- **[lemma_2]** (lemma, by planner) Witten Laplacian Unitary Isomorphism for Norm-Preserving Resolvent Analysis: The unitary map U: L^2(pi) -> L^2(dx), U(f) = f e^{-V(x)/2} unitarily transforms generator -L to non-self-adjoint Schrodinger operator H_J = -Δ + (1/4)|∇V|^2 - (1/2)ΔV + J ∇V ≡ ∇ in flat L^2(dx). Resolvent operator norms satisfy ||(Η I - L)^{-1}||_{L^2(pi)} = ||(Η I + H_J)^{-1}||_{L^2(dx)}, enabling exact resolvent and pseudospectral convergence analysis for unnormalized targets without knowledge of normalization constant Z.
- **[lemma_3]** (lemma, by planner) Continuous Resolvent Operator Duality & L^2(pi) Contraction: For Langevin diffusion generator L = Δ - ∇V · ∇ on unnormalized target π ∝ e^{-V}, the continuous resolvent operator R_γ = (I - γ L)^{-1} acts on f ∈ L^2(π) as R_γ f(x) = E_x[f(X_τ)] where τ ~ Exp(1/γ). Because -L is positive semi-definite, R_γ is a self-adjoint contraction on L^2(π) with operator norm ||R_γ||_{L^2(π) -> L^2(π)} ≤ (1 + γ λ_1)^{-1} on zero-mean functions. Discrete implicit updates (e.g. Moreau-Yosida envelope steps) inherit this spectral contraction without requiring evaluation of the normalization constant Z.
- **[lemma_4]** (lemma, by planner) Moreau-Yosida Proximal Resolvent Bias Bound: For non-smooth target potential V = U + g where U is L-smooth and g is M-Lipschitz convex, replacing V with its Moreau-Yosida envelope V_lambda(x) = U(x) + inf_y {g(y) + (1/(2 lambda)) ||x - y||^2} yields a smooth gradient grad V_lambda(x) = grad U(x) + (1/lambda)(x - prox_{lambda g}(x)). The biased stationary distribution pi_lambda propto e^{-V_lambda} satisfies TV(pi_lambda, pi) <= lambda M^2 / 2, guaranteeing that proximal resolvent discretization achieves convergence on non-smooth unnormalized potentials with controlled O(lambda) asymptotic bias without needing the normalization constant Z.
- **[approach_5]** (approach, by planner) Dual-Splitting Non-Reversible Resolvent Sampler: Approximates non-reversible resolvent step (I - gamma L_J)^{-1} via Strang composition (I - (gamma/2) L_{rot})^{-1} (I - gamma L_{rev})^{-1} (I - (gamma/2) L_{rot})^{-1}. The rotational step uses an implicit midpoint step x_{k+1} = x_k - (gamma/2) J grad V((x_k + x_{k+1})/2) which is volume-preserving. For quadratic targets V(x) = (1/2) x^T S x with optimal J*, discrete resolvent conditioning collapses from kappa(S) to 1 at step size gamma approx 2/s_bar.
- **[approach_6]** (approach, by planner) Streaming Finite-Difference Krylov Construction for Non-Reversible Drift J(x): Estimates local Hessian action S(x)v approx (grad V(x + epsilon v) - grad V(x - epsilon v))/(2 epsilon) along a low-rank m-dimensional Krylov subspace (m << d), avoiding explicit O(d^2) Hessian matrix computation while dynamically constructing optimal non-reversible drift J(x) for unnormalized targets.
- **[lemma_5]** (lemma, by planner) Unconditional L^2(pi) Discrete Resolvent Contraction Bound: For any positive semi-definite MCMC generator A = -L on L^2(pi) with spectral gap lambda_{gap} > 0, the discrete proximal resolvent operator J_gamma = (I + gamma A)^{-1} satisfies ||J_gamma||_{L_0^2(pi) -> L_0^2(pi)} <= (1 + 2 gamma lambda_{gap})^{-1/2} <= 1 - gamma lambda_{gap} + O(gamma^2) for all gamma > 0. This guarantees strict operator contraction for arbitrary step sizes without path-integral trajectory bias explosion.
- **[approach_7]** (approach, by planner) Control-Variate Residual Resolvent Monte Carlo (CR-RMC): Accelerate stochastic trajectory estimation of resolvent action (\lambda I - L)^{-1} f by choosing a surrogate function \hat{g} \approx (\lambda I - L)^{-1} f. Computing (\lambda I - L)^{-1} f = \hat{g} + (\lambda I - L)^{-1}(f - (\lambda I - L)\hat{g}) reduces variance and trajectory bias by a factor of ||f - (\lambda I - L)\hat{g}|| / ||f||, bypassing knowledge of the normalization constant Z because L \hat{g} = \Delta \hat{g} - \nabla V \cdot \nabla \hat{g} depends only on unnormalized target gradients \nabla V.
- **[idea_1]** (idea, by planner) Magnetic Vector Potential Representation of Non-Reversible Drift: In d=2, the non-reversible drift vector field A(x) = J(x) grad V(x) with antisymmetric J(x) = [[0, j(x)], [-j(x), 0]] acts as a vector potential. Its curl, representing an effective perpendicular magnetic field, is B(x) = curl A = -div(j(x) grad V(x)). For constant j(x) = j, B(x) = -j div(grad V) = -j Delta V(x). This suggests that non-reversible drift accelerates convergence by introducing a rotational 'magnetic' force proportional to the Laplacian of the potential, driving the sampler away from saddle points and breaking metastable traps.
- **[lemma_7]** (lemma, by planner) Non-Reversible Resolvent Coercivity vs Spectral Gap Paradox in L^2(pi): For any non-reversible Langevin generator L_J = L_0 - J grad V . grad with skew-adjoint perturbation J, the numerical range is constrained by the reversible generator: Re <u, L_J u>_pi = <u, L_0 u>_pi >= m ||u||_pi^2 for zero-mean u in L^2(pi), where m is the reversible spectral gap. This implies that the discrete resolvent norm in L^2(pi) satisfies ||(I + gamma L_J)^(-1)||_(L^2(pi)) <= (1 + gamma m)^(-1), which is identical to the reversible rate and fails to show any acceleration. However, the spectral gap lambda_gap(L_J) = inf Re(sigma(L_J)) can be strictly larger than m. This demonstrates that L^2(pi) is too coarse a space/norm to capture non-reversible acceleration, and one must use custom hypocoercive norms or pseudospectral resolvent bounds to analyze the transient and asymptotic speedup.
- **[lemma_8]** (lemma, by planner) Witten-Dirichlet Isomorphism on Bounded Domains: The unitary transformation U_\Omega: L^2(\Omega, \pi|_\Omega) -> L^2(\Omega, dx) defined by U_\Omega f = f e^{-V/2} unitarily transforms the Dirichlet generator L_\Omega = \Delta - \nabla V \cdot \nabla + J \nabla V \cdot \nabla$ on a bounded domain \Omega with boundary condition f|_{\partial\Omega} = 0 to the flat-space non-self-adjoint Schrodinger operator H_{\Omega, J} = -\Delta + V_{eff} + J \nabla V \cdot \nabla with V_{eff} = (1/4)|\nabla V|^2 - (1/2)\Delta V and zero Dirichlet boundary conditions. This preserves the resolvent norm perfectly, enabling localized pseudospectral and metastability analysis without requiring the global normalization constant Z.
- **[lemma_9]** (lemma, by planner) Skew-Symmetric Tensor Construction for State-Dependent Non-Reversible Drift: To resolve the stationary distribution distortion of state-dependent non-reversible drift, let $\Gamma(x)$ be any smooth, state-dependent skew-symmetric matrix field (i.e., $\Gamma(x)^T = -\Gamma(x)$ for all $x$). Define the non-reversible drift vector field $u(x)$ component-wise by $u_i(x) = \sum_{j=1}^d [ \partial_j \Gamma_{ij}(x) - \Gamma_{ij}(x) \partial_j V(x) ]$. Then the Fokker-Planck adjoint equation $L^* \pi = 0$ is satisfied identically for $\pi \propto e^{-V(x)}$, since the probability flux $u \pi = \operatorname{div}(\Gamma \pi)$ has zero divergence ($\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{div}(\Gamma \pi)) = 0$ by symmetry of mixed partials and antisymmetry of $\Gamma$). This provides a general, normalization-free recipe for designing state-dependent non-reversible samplers.
- **[approach_8]** (approach, by planner) Stochastic Resolvent Sampling via Exponential Time-Stepping (SRS-ETS): A training-free, normalization-free MCMC method to simulate the exact resolvent operator R_\gamma = (I - \gamma L)^{-1} of the Langevin diffusion. For a current state X_k, we sample a random duration \tau_k \sim \operatorname{Exp}(1/\gamma) and run the Langevin SDE dX_t = -\nabla V(X_t) dt + \sqrt{2} dW_t starting at X_k for time t \in [0, \tau_k], setting X_{k+1} = X_{\tau_k}. Because the continuous-time SDE preserves \pi \propto e^{-V} at all times and requires only the gradient \nabla V(x), this exact resolvent transition step preserves the target distribution exactly and inherits the unconditional L^2(\pi) contraction property (lemma_5) without requiring the normalization constant Z or Metropolis-Hastings acceptance steps.
- **[approach_9]** (approach, by planner) Taxonomic MCMC Classification via Witten-transformed Resolvent Pseudospectra: MCMC generators L on state space X are classified based on the non-normality of their Witten-transformed operators H = U L U^{-1} in flat L^2(dx) and the topology of their pseudospectra \sigma_\epsilon(L). Reversible diffusion generators (ULA, MALA) yield self-adjoint H (normal operators) where the resolvent norm satisfies ||(\lambda I - L)^{-1}|| = dist(\lambda, \sigma(L))^{-1}. Non-reversible diffusion generators yield non-normal H with highly expanded pseudospectra, allowing rapid transient decay. Second-order (HMC) and PDMPs (Bouncy Particle Sampler, Zig-Zag) generate non-local or integrodifferential operators with even more severe non-normality. This entire taxonomy is independent of the normalization constant Z as H depends only on \nabla V and \Delta V.

## Obstacles

- **[obstacle_1]** (obstacle, by planner) Computational Intractability of High-Dimensional Resolvents: Direct inversion or evaluation of (\lambda I - L)^{-1} in high dimensions requires solving high-dimensional PDEs or linear systems, necessitating randomized or stochastic operator approximations.
- **[obstacle_2]** (obstacle, by planner) Localized Dirichlet resolvent methods (lambda I - L_Omega)^{-1} fail to reliably distinguish metastable basins because for practical choices of lambda >> lambda_1, the resolvent norm is capped at lambda^{-1}, rendering it uniform across all basins with barrier heights exceeding epsilon log(1/lambda). Furthermore, the unitary Witten Laplacian transformation breaks down on bounded domains with Dirichlet boundaries due to boundary term artifacts, failing to preserve the norm equivalence of the resolvent.
- **[obstacle_3]** (obstacle, by planner) Stationary Distribution Distortion under State-Dependent Drift: For a state-dependent antisymmetric matrix $J(x)$, the non-reversible drift $b(x) = -(I + J(x))\nabla V(x)$ preserves the target distribution $\pi \propto e^{-V}$ if and only if the vector field $A(x) = J(x) \nabla V(x)$ is divergence-free with respect to $\pi$, which simplifies to $\operatorname{div}(J(x) \nabla V(x)) = 0$. A naive state-dependent construction like in approach_4 (e.g., local Gram-Schmidt or Hessian-based $J(x)$) violates this condition (for instance, $J(x) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & x_1 \\ -x_1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ on $V(x) = \frac{1}{2}|x|^2$ yields $\operatorname{div}(J \nabla V) = x_2 \neq 0$), leading to severe stationary distribution bias.

## Open ideas and questions

- **[approach_3]** (approach, by planner) Resolvent Probing for Metastability: Detect metastable states and spectral clustering using resolvent kernels (\lambda I - L)^{-1} f evaluated at small \lambda > 0. High resolvent response indicates long dwell times in metastable basins.
- **[lemma_6]** (lemma, by planner) Witten Laplacian 1-Particle Sector Isomorphism for Quadratic Targets: For a quadratic potential V(x) = (1/2)x^T S x with S > 0 and a skew-symmetric matrix J, the Witten Laplacian operator H_J = -\Delta + (1/4)|\nabla V|^2 - (1/2)\Delta V + J \nabla V (which is unitarily equivalent to the non-reversible Langevin generator) preserves the 1-particle subspace W_1 = \operatorname{span}\{x_1 e^{-V(x)/2}, \dots, x_d e^{-V(x)/2}\}. On W_1, the action of H_J is unitarily isomorphic to the action of the finite-dimensional drift matrix A = (I+J)S on \mathbb{R}^d. This establishes that the pseudospectral and resolvent behavior of the infinite-dimensional operator on this subspace is exactly given by the d-dimensional matrix A, linking finite-dimensional linear analysis directly to the MCMC convergence rates.
- **[lemma_10]** (lemma, by planner) Commutator Hypocoercivity and Resolvent Bounds: For the Witten-transformed Langevin operator with non-reversible drift H_J = H_0 + H_A, where H_0 = -\Delta + V_{eff} is self-adjoint and H_A = J \nabla V \cdot \nabla + (1/2)\operatorname{div}(J \nabla V) is skew-adjoint, the commutator [H_0, H_A] is self-adjoint. Under conditions where [H_0, H_A] satisfies a positive lower bound on the orthogonal complement of the ground state, the resolvent operator norm of (\lambda I - H_J)^{-1} is significantly contracted compared to the reversible case. This provides a normalization-free criterion to optimize the non-reversible perturbation J(x).
