## Findings

1. **The Circularity Bottleneck: Resolvent Stochastic Estimation Requires Traversing the Metastable Barrier It Aims to Detect**
   Evaluating the resolvent action $(\lambda I - L)^{-1} f(x)$ via Laplace transform of the Markov semigroup, $(\lambda I - L)^{-1} f(x) = \int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t} \mathbb{E}_x [f(X_t)] \, dt$, requires simulating trajectories $X_t$ up to time $T \sim O(1/\lambda)$. To detect a metastable state with spectral gap $\lambda_2$, one must evaluate the resolvent at $\lambda < \lambda_2$. By the Eyring-Kramers law (e.g., for $dV(x) = (x^2-1)^2 dx$ with noise $\sigma$), the transition time across a barrier of height $U$ scales as $\tau_{\text{Kramers}} \propto e^{U/\sigma^2}$, so $\lambda_2 \sim e^{-U/\sigma^2}$. Any finite-horizon Monte Carlo estimator of $\int_0^T e^{-\lambda t} f(X_t) \, dt$ initialized in a metastable well with $T \ll \tau_{\text{Kramers}}$ will sample only the local quasi-stationary distribution. The stochastic estimator yields $(\lambda I - L_{\text{local}})^{-1} f(x)$, completely missing the global spectrum and false-positive reporting high resolvent norm for local reasons. Thus, using empirical resolvents to *discover* unknown metastability is circular: it requires sample paths that have already mixed across the barrier.

2. **False Positives: Pseudospectral Blowup in Non-Reversible Samplers Without Metastability**
   Approach 1 relies on the pseudospectrum $\sigma_\varepsilon(L) = \{\lambda \in \mathbb{C} : \|(\lambda I - L)^{-1}\|_{L^2(\pi)} > 1/\varepsilon\}$ to characterize convergence and metastability. For non-reversible Langevin diffusions $dX_t = -(\nabla V(X_t) + J \nabla V(X_t)) dt + \sqrt{2} dW_t$ with anti-symmetric matrix $J = -J^T$, $L$ is non-normal. Consider the 2D harmonic oscillator $V(x,y) = \frac{1}{2}(x^2 + y^2)$ with $J = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \gamma \\ -\gamma & 0 \end{pmatrix}$. The potential has a single global minimum (zero metastability), and the spectral gap is $\text{Re}(\lambda_2) = 1$ for all $\gamma$. However, as the non-reversible circulation parameter $\gamma \to \infty$, the pseudospectrum $\sigma_\varepsilon(L)$ expands arbitrarily far into the complex plane along imaginary directions. An observer using large resolvent norm $\|(\lambda I - L)^{-1}\|$ as a diagnostic for metastability would incorrectly flag this rapidly mixing, single-well Gaussian system as highly metastable.

3. **Inaccessibility of the $L^2(\pi)$ Norm for Unnormalized Posteriors**
   The resolvent operator norm $\|(\lambda I - L)^{-1}\|_{L^2(\pi)} = \sup_{\|f\|_{L^2(\pi)}=1} \|(\lambda I - L)^{-1} f\|_{L^2(\pi)}$ is defined relative to the weighted Hilbert space $L^2(\pi)$ with inner product $\langle f, g \rangle_{\pi} = \int f(x) g(x) \pi(x) dx$. When $\pi(x) = Z^{-1} e^{-V(x)}$ has an unknown normalization constant $Z = \int e^{-V(x)} dx$, one cannot evaluate the norm $\|f\|_{L^2(\pi)}$ directly. While unitarily transforming to the flat space $L^2(dx)$ via $H = e^{V/2} L e^{-V/2} = \Delta - \left(\frac{1}{4}|\nabla V|^2 - \frac{1}{2}\Delta V\right)$ removes $\pi$ from the inner product, this transformation requires explicit, bounded global control over $\Delta V$ and $|\nabla V|^2$. For high-dimensional complex targets (e.g., deep generative priors or Bayesian neural networks), $\Delta V$ is computationally intractable ($O(d^2)$ trace of Hessian) or unbounded, rendering the standard resolvent norm uncomputable and theoretical bounds unquantifiable.

4. **Breakdown of Proximal/Implicit Resolvent Steps for Non-Convex $V(x)$**
   Approach 2 proposes discrete steps via the Yosida approximation or proximal resolvent $(I - \gamma L)^{-1}$. By the Hille-Yosida Theorem, $(I - \gamma L)^{-1}$ is a well-defined non-expansive contraction on $L^2(\pi)$ if and only if $L$ is m-dissipative ($\langle L f, f \rangle_\pi \le 0$ and $\text{Range}(I - \gamma L) = L^2(\pi)$). For a drift operator $L = -\nabla V \cdot \nabla + \Delta$, if $V(x)$ is non-convex (the primary scenario for metastable posteriors), the corresponding deterministic proximal step $\text{prox}_{\gamma V}(x) = \arg\min_y \left\{ V(y) + \frac{1}{2\gamma} \|x - y\|^2 \right\}$ becomes multi-valued for $\gamma > 1/\text{Lipschitz}(\nabla V)$. In multi-well potentials, $(I - \gamma L)^{-1}$ applied to localized test functions creates discontinuous or multi-branched solutions, causing numerical implicit resolvent solvers (like fixed-point iteration or Newton-Raphson) to diverge or oscillate unpredictably between wells.

## Obstacles

* **High-Dimensional Trace Computation:** Transforming $L$ to a self-adjoint Schrödinger operator $H = \Delta - W(x)$ to avoid unknown $\pi$ requires $W(x) = \frac{1}{4}|\nabla V(x)|^2 - \frac{1}{2}\Delta V(x)$. In dimension $d = 10^4$, computing $\Delta V(x) = \text{Tr}(\nabla^2 V(x))$ at every MCMC step requires $O(d)$ automatic differentiation passes or Hutchinson trace estimation, introducing severe stochastic variance into the resolvent evaluation.
* **Variance Explosion in Laplace Integrals:** The estimator $\hat{R}_\lambda f(x) = \int_0^T e^{-\lambda t} f(X_t) dt$ has variance $\text{Var}_x(\hat{R}_\lambda f) \approx \int_0^T \int_0^T e^{-\lambda(t+s)} \text{Cov}_x(f(X_t), f(X_s)) dt ds$. In metastable regimes, $\text{Cov}_x(f(X_t), f(X_s))$ remains $O(1)$ for $t, s < \tau_{\text{Kramers}}$, causing the variance of $\hat{R}_\lambda$ to grow as $O(1/\lambda^2)$, completely drowning out the signal when $\lambda \to 0^+$.
* **Failure of Kreiss Matrix Theorem in Infinite Dimensions:** Bounds on the resolvent $\|(\lambda I - L)^{-1}\| \le \frac{M}{\text{Re}(\lambda)}$ guarantee semigroup bounds $\|e^{tL}\| \le M e$ (Kreiss Theorem). However, in high dimensions $d \to \infty$, the constant $M = M(d)$ can scale exponentially as $e^{O(d)}$, making resolvent bounds completely uninformative for non-asymptotic, finite-time MCMC convergence.
